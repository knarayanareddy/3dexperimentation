## Creative idea

Follow a cheerful, generic cargo-box mascot on a hand-drawn map. A land ribbon and a sea ribbon fork, reconnect, and take the audience past rail, port, customs, market, ledger, and local meeting-table icons. The map is a **schematic, not to scale**; it is deliberately not a geographic route map and does not imply one continuous road, one fixed project list, or a uniform outcome.

The energy comes from the mascot's paper-map mishaps, bouncing customs stamps, shifting route ribbons, and an upbeat travelogue groove. The risks are not punchlines: debt, procurement, land, labor, environmental safeguards, and local decision-making get calm, legible frames. The close invites viewers to ask who pays, benefits, and decides.

## Editorial spine and evidence rules

1. **Origin and terminology:** China proposed the Silk Road Economic Belt in September 2013 and the 21st Century Maritime Silk Road in October 2013. The BRI is an umbrella initiative/framework with diverse links and projects—not one road or one uniform project. Attribute aims and official descriptions to the Chinese government.
2. **Official framing:** China's 2023 white paper describes six overland corridors and uses language including “extensive consultation, joint contribution and shared benefits.” Present that as a stated principle, not an audited outcome.
3. **Conditional benefits:** the World Bank's 2019 analysis models potential impacts of transport corridors. It estimates that travel times along corridors could decline by **up to 12% once projects are completed**. The video labels this “World Bank model · 2019 · if completed · corridors · not observed.” It is a projection, not a measured current result or a guarantee for any specific project.
4. **Risks and conditions:** the World Bank discusses fiscal/debt, transparency, procurement, corruption, environmental, and social risks; it says potential benefits depend on complementary policy and institutional reforms. Infrastructure can also produce uneven gains, costs, or losses. Do not imply that a projected aggregate benefit automatically reaches every community.
5. **Independent historical evidence:** AidData's 2021 report says **35% of the BRI infrastructure project portfolio in its study** encountered major implementation problems. Its underlying Chinese development-finance dataset covers 13,427 projects worth $843 billion across 165 countries from 2000–2017; that broad dataset is **not all BRI**. The 35% figure refers to the report's BRI-infrastructure subset and is a dated study estimate, not a current census of every BRI project. The report describes issues including corruption scandals, labor violations, environmental hazards, and public protests.
6. **Debt nuance:** debt exposure and repayment opacity are serious issues to assess, but loans, borrowers, contract terms, collateral, and project outcomes differ. Do not claim that every Chinese loan is a “debt trap,” that every project is debt-financed, or that the 35% figure proves a debt-trap thesis. Avoid naming a country/project without doing a separate project-level evidence review.
7. **Map and counts:** route ribbons and map-like shapes are illustrative, not a route dataset. The World Bank's 2019 report notes that there was no official participant-country list and that geographic corridor definitions and signed cooperation agreements produced different country sets. Do not show a single definitive membership total or a live project counter.
8. **Currency and dates:** the research cut is intentionally anchored to dated primary/independent material (China white paper 2023; World Bank report 2019; AidData report 2021/data through 2017). Do not update old estimates into present-tense “today” claims without new evidence.

**Claim ledger for the film:**

- “Two thousand thirteen” refers to the two proposals China announced in September and October 2013; lyric compression combines these two dates into one year.
- “Six corridors” refers to the six overland economic corridors in the Chinese government's 2023 white paper. It does not mean that every BRI project lies on one of six precise lines.
- “Up to twelve percent lower” refers to the World Bank's **modelled maximum corridor travel-time reduction once completed**, not 12% for every route and not a measured result.
- “Thirty-five percent” refers to AidData's 2021 finding for its BRI infrastructure project portfolio. The on-screen label includes study year, historical data cutoff, and warning that it is not all BRI today.
- “Shared benefits” appears only with “China's stated framing · 2023”; the next visual explicitly asks who decides and who benefits.
- “Debt risk is real” is followed by “one label can't explain all,” a project/borrower/terms comparison, and a local-voice prompt. No country is singled out or reduced to a cautionary prop.

### Research detail deliberately left out of the 108-second cut

- The World Bank report also models corridor-economy trade growth of 2.8–9.7% and global trade growth of 1.7–6.2%, as well as potential poverty reductions of 7.6 million people in extreme poverty and 32 million in moderate poverty. These are conditional model estimates, not observed totals; the poverty thresholds use the report's 2011-PPP-era definitions. Omitted from the short to avoid crowding several modelled outcomes into one fast stat montage.
- AidData reports 42 low-/middle-income countries with China debt exposure above 10% of GDP and estimates about $385 billion in underreported actual or potential repayment obligations, equivalent to an average 5.8% of GDP. These are AidData's historical estimates for China's wider overseas development-finance activity, not BRI-only current debt totals. Omitted from the short because the methodological scope and attribution need more time than a quick on-screen number allows.
- The AidData analysis also describes collateralized lending and a high loan-to-grant ratio in China's overseas development finance; neither fact on its own establishes that a particular borrower was deliberately pushed into default or that a specific project was a “debt trap.” The cut does not use that label as a blanket explanation.

## Visual, sound, and accessibility direction

- **Palette:** paper cream, rust, teal, muted blue, mustard, plum, sage, charcoal. Hand-drawn linework, rounded paper labels, small route confetti.
- **Motion:** 2D Canvas, gentle map drift, route-dash animation, ship bob, train glide, bouncing stamps, mascot squash/bounce. Keep all factual labels on screen long enough to read on a phone.
- **Character:** one generic cargo-box mascot; no flag, national costume, logo, or claimed point of view. Do not use it to trivialize communities, debt, or environmental impacts.
- **Maps:** abstract blobs and schematic ribbons only. “SCHEMATIC · NOT TO SCALE” remains visible. Never use an invented border, exact corridor line, project location, or participant-country map as if factual.
- **Evidence labels:** use readable contrast and keep caveats adjacent to the number, not hidden in a credits-only footnote. The 12% card and 35% card must each be understandable as still images.
- **Captions:** lyric is displayed in a high-contrast, lower safe-area panel; the research qualifiers are also on-screen, not only in the production notes. The bundled Suno song plays by default; the sync map is still provisional and should be auditioned.
- **End credit:** include source names, years, and short links/QR or a linked source slate in the published cut. Keep “China's stated framing” visibly distinct from independent World Bank/AidData findings.
