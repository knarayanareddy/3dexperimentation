## The thesis

AI infrastructure financing is increasingly interconnected: companies invest in AI labs or cloud providers, while those labs make multi-year compute or infrastructure commitments to the same ecosystem. Nvidia is an especially visible hub because it supplies GPUs and also participates as an investor and, in selected contracts, a capacity buyer. This creates a legitimate question about how resilient demand, valuations, and debt would be if expected AI returns disappointed.

The story is **not** that every deal is a closed cash loop, that revenue is fictitious, or that a crash is inevitable. Equity investment, a purchase commitment, delivered services, recognized revenue, debt, and cash are different things. The physical build-out—chips, data centers, power, cooling, and construction—is real. The risk is the degree of concentration, interdependence, leverage, and reliance on future utilization and returns.

## Research notes and fact checks (as of 28 October 2026)

### Nvidia and OpenAI: do not merge the headlines

- OpenAI's September 22, 2025 primary announcement said Nvidia intended to invest up to $100B progressively as systems were deployed; the companies described a planned 10 GW deployment. Reuters described the signed document as a letter of intent. Treat the headline as an intended framework, not as money already transferred.
- Bloomberg's October 27, 2026 report says Nvidia ultimately contributed about $30B in an OpenAI funding round, separately from the 2025 framework. CNBC's February 2026 report had described talks for an investment of up to $30B and said it was separate from the earlier framework; at that time it was not final. The later Bloomberg report is the source for the subsequent outcome used in this film.
- The script's joke is the shifting scale of the headlines, not an allegation that OpenAI paid Nvidia back the same investment dollars.

### Microsoft / Azure and Oracle

- Microsoft's October 28, 2025 definitive-partnership announcement said OpenAI contracted to buy an **incremental $250B of Azure services**. Microsoft's April 27, 2026 partnership update amended other terms and broadened cloud flexibility; the short dates the $250B figure to the October 2025 announcement rather than suggesting every term stayed frozen.
- OpenAI's July 22, 2025 Stargate post said the Oracle agreement would develop up to 4.5 GW of additional data-center capacity. OpenAI's September 23, 2025 update described the partnership as exceeding $300B over five years. The short uses “about/over $300B over five years” as a reported scale, not a same-day cash payment.

### CoreWeave / Nvidia: quote the contract structure

- CoreWeave's September 15, 2025 SEC Form 8-K describes a September 9 order form with an initial value of $6.3B. Where CoreWeave capacity is not fully used by its own customers, Nvidia is obligated to buy residual unsold capacity through April 13, 2032, subject to delivery and availability requirements and termination provisions.
- The filing separately says Nvidia supplies CoreWeave GPUs and is a stockholder. The order form is a conditional residual-capacity purchase arrangement. Do **not** call it an unlimited guarantee, blanket debt backstop, or proof of guaranteed demand at every site.

### What regulators and central bankers actually warn about

- The IMF's October 2026 GFSR blog reports that its review of 14 transactions since 2023 found approximately $300B in equity investments and $650B in debt exchanged alongside $1.4T in future revenue commitments. It says stress at key recipients could weaken revenue forecasts and investment values and reduce the value of accumulated deal debt. It frames this as vulnerability and interconnectedness, not proof of fabricated revenue or a dated crash prediction.
- The BIS Annual Economic Report 2026 describes large AI-related capex, growing debt finance, concentrated exposures, and increasingly circular financing. It explains that an investment pullback could hurt borrowers dependent on hyperscaler spending, while also acknowledging AI's real contribution to investment and growth. It presents downside scenarios, not certainty.
- Counterweight: OpenAI and Anthropic products have substantial reported user/customer adoption and the sector is building tangible infrastructure. Stronger monetization and productivity gains could sustain the build-out. The film leaves that possibility open.
